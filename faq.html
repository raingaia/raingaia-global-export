The company & products

Are Network, Seismos, and Bio the same product?

No. They're three independent products built by the same company, monitoring three different physical signals — network traffic, structural vibration, and bioelectric activity. They share a reasoning discipline (see our technical approach), not a codebase — each has its own engine, its own database, and its own account system.

Is my data shared between the three products, or with other customers?

No. Each product's customers are isolated from each other, and the three products don't share data with one another. Raingaia Network, for example, provisions each paying customer their own analysis engine and database — never a shared pool.

How established is RAINGAIA as a company?

We're a small, early-stage, Florida-based team. Raingaia Network is our most commercially active product today. We'd rather be direct about our stage than overstate it — see About, and ask us directly about scale or deployment history if you're evaluating us for something important.

The methodology

Does RAINGAIA actually use quantum computing?

No. "Quantum-probabilistic" refers to borrowing the mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics — state vectors, density operators, projective measurement, entropy — to reason about uncertainty. Every product runs as ordinary software on standard CPUs; no quantum hardware is involved anywhere. See our technical approach for the full explanation.

Is this a machine-learning model?

No, not in the "trained neural network" sense. The core is classical statistical signal processing and a probabilistic decision layer — source-relative baselines, multi-channel fusion, entropy-based uncertainty. It's deterministic by design: the same input always produces the same output, which is what makes a decision auditable.

What does "threshold-free" actually mean?

No stage in the decision layer holds a fixed magic-number cutoff (like "if score > 0.8, alert"). A verdict is an argmax over a source-relative probability distribution built from that source's own history instead — the threshold effectively adapts to what's normal for that specific source.
