The problem with single-signal monitoring

Most structural monitoring software reads one signal type in isolation — vibration alone, or strain alone — and reports it as a chart someone still has to interpret. Raingaia Seismos instead cross-checks four independent signal types against each other: vibration, stiffness (frequency drift), strain, and tilt/settlement. A real structural issue tends to show up in more than one of them; a sensor artifact usually doesn't.

How it works

Sensor data is uploaded, analyzed by the same evidence-fusion engine described in our technical approach, and returned as a verdict and a report: a stiffness-loss estimate, a damage-equivalent volume, and an inspection priority — not just a signal-analysis chart someone still has to interpret. The pipeline: upload sensor data → the engine analyzes it → you get a verdict and report → you act on the recommendation → the structure stays monitored going forward.

Grounded in published methods

The stiffness-loss and damage-equivalence estimates are grounded in published structural engineering research (Worden et al. 2012; Salawu 1997) rather than an unpublished black-box scoring model — the underlying methods are ones a structural engineer can look up and evaluate independently.

What the console shows

Both an engineering view and a facilities-manager view are available: dashboard, buckling/torsion risk, critical alerts, vibration, infrastructure overview, strain gauge, and tilt/settlement — real telemetry from all four signal types, for technical staff and integrators alike.

Who it's for

Facilities managers, structural engineers, and asset owners responsible for bridges, buildings, or other structures who need a continuous, evidence-based health signal instead of a periodic manual inspection alone.
